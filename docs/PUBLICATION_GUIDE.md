## Journal preparation

The [Quantum Machine Intelligence instructions](https://link.springer.com/journal/42484/submission-guidelines) require editable manuscript sources, author details and declarations. Mathematical manuscripts may use LaTeX. Its abstract range is 150–250 words and it requests 4–6 keywords. All authors must approve submission, and the work cannot simultaneously be under consideration elsewhere. AI use beyond copy editing must be documented; accountability remains with human authors. The current manuscript contains an assistance disclosure and must receive substantive human review.

The [publisher's options](https://link.springer.com/journal/42484/how-to-publish-with-us) include subscription publication without an article-processing charge and optional paid open access. The displayed open-access price depends on acceptance date and taxes; no paid route is selected here. Conference registration/presentation costs are separate. Recheck the official pages before committing funds or submitting.

The current paper is a review draft. Its general presentation is not claimed to satisfy every venue-specific citation/template requirement. Final author names, affiliations, contributions, competing interests, funding and repository licensing must reflect actual facts. Do not fill them with invented declarations. Maintain a dated final literature search, especially for close QUBO/RAG work, before claiming novelty.

## Submission steps

1. Review methods, saved question-level predictions, statistical assumptions, limitations and the claim-to-evidence map. Resolve substantive errors before formatting.
2. Agree the author list/order, corresponding author, affiliations, contributions and any institutional requirements. Each author reviews and accepts responsibility for the final content.
3. Choose one venue and article type. Apply its current editable template, citation style, anonymity policy, word/page limits and disclosure rules. Verify all references against primary sources.
4. Choose code/artifact and manuscript licenses with the rights holders. Check third-party dataset/model terms. Create an archival artifact version if appropriate; a GitHub tag alone is not a preservation guarantee or an awarded reproducibility badge.
5. Upload through the venue's official submission link. Supply the manuscript, editable source, figures, supplement, availability statement and required declarations. Check the generated submission PDF before final confirmation.
6. Retain the submission receipt. If reviews arrive, answer them point by point and distinguish new experiments from the frozen original evaluation. Acceptance, proofs, registration and presentation may add later work.

No portal submission, email, reviewer nomination, authorship agreement or payment is performed by this implementation run.

## Preprint and timeline

An arXiv posting is a preprint, not peer-reviewed acceptance. [Official submission guidance](https://github.com/arXiv/arxiv-docs/blob/develop/source/help/submit/index.md) requires a registered submitting author, appropriate scientific content and a distribution license. [Endorsement guidance](https://github.com/arXiv/arxiv-docs/blob/develop/source/help/endorsement.md) explains that new categories/authors may require endorsement. Follow the chosen venue's preprint/anonymity policy and obtain coauthor agreement before posting.

There is no fixed project deadline. Use completion criteria: finished audited experiments, defensible interpretation, independently reviewed manuscript and verified submission files. Allow real time for review and corrections. Journal peer review has no guaranteed acceptance date; conference dates are submission opportunities, not reasons to lower the evidence standard. The historical schedule in [the implementation plan](IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md) is superseded by this completion-first policy.
